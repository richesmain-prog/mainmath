Lesson 1 · Step 1 in the app · Spec Build

Is it a good deal?

You will find out if Maple Street makes money, and the most you should pay for the lot.

1Type the deal

👉 Do this In Deal name, type Maple Street practice
In Lot purchase price, type 200000
In Home size (sqft), type 2000
In Build cost / sqft, type 150
In Sale price / sqft, type 400
Leave Build months at 12 and Months to sell at 2.
💡 Why it mattersThese four numbers are all the app needs to judge a deal. It fills in the rest with sample numbers.

2Read the two big numbers

👉 Do thisLook at the top of the page.
👀 You should seeProfit margin 13.4%. The word Tight. Deal score 68.
💡 Why it mattersMargin is the part of the sale price you keep. Your goal is 20%. At $200,000 for the lot, you only keep 13.4%. That is not enough.
🍋 ExampleYou run a lemonade stand. You sell a cup for $1.00. The lemons, sugar and cup cost you 80¢. You keep 20¢. Your margin is 20%. If your costs were 87¢, you would keep only 13¢. That is a tight stand.

3Read the gold box

👉 Do thisScroll down to the gold box.
👀 You should see$153,448. Under it: the asking price is $46,552 above the price that still hits 20%.
💡 Why it mattersThis is the most you should pay for the lot. The seller wants $200,000. That is too much. Now you know what to offer.
🍋 ExampleYou fix up old bikes and sell them for $100. The fixing costs $50. You want to keep $20. So the most you can pay for an old bike is $30. If the owner wants $45, it is too much.

4Make a lower offer

👉 Do thisChange Lot purchase price to 150000
👀 You should seeMargin 20.5%. The words Strong Deal. Score 🏆 80. Confetti falls. Profit went from $107,443 to $163,893.
💡 Why it mattersNothing about the house changed. Only the land price changed. A lower land price turned a tight deal into a strong one.
🍋 ExampleThe bike owner wanted $45. You offered $30, and they said yes. The fixing still costs $50. The bike still sells for $100. But now you keep $20, not $5.

5See where the money goes

👉 Do thisScroll to the block 🧾 Where the money goes. Read it from top to bottom.
👀 You should seeThe top line is the sale: $800,000. That is $400 × 2,000 sqft. Each red line takes a cost away. The last line is Net profit $163,893.
💡 Why it mattersThe bar shows the slices. Land is 19%. Building is 46%. Your profit is 20%.
🍋 ExampleCut a pizza into 10 slices. About 2 slices pay for the land. Almost 5 pay for building. About 1 pays the bank and the bills while you wait. A little more than half a slice pays for selling. The 2 slices left are yours.

6Try a bigger goal

👉 Do thisIn the gold box, tap 25%.
👀 You should seeThe gold number drops to $118,018. The word at the top goes back to Tight.
💡 Why it mattersIf you want more profit, you must pay less for the land. Now tap 20% to go back.
🍋 ExampleNow you want to keep $25 on each bike, not $20. The bike still sells for $100 and fixing still costs $50. So the most you can pay drops from $30 to $25.

7Test a bad day

👉 Do thisTap What if costs or prices change? to open it. Find the row $165 and the column $360.
👀 You should see7.2% in that square. Your deal today is the middle square: 20.5%.
💡 Why it mattersIf building costs go up to $165 and the sale price drops to $360, your margin falls from 20.5% to 7.2%. Always check the bad day before you buy.
🍋 ExampleIt rains on your lemonade stand, so you drop your price to 90¢. The same week, lemons cost more, so a cup costs you 85¢ to make. Now you keep 5¢, not 20¢.

8Save your deal

👉 Do thisTap Save deal. Then tap Saved at the top of the page.
👀 You should seeA message: Saved “Maple Street practice”. Your deal is in the list. Tap Close.
💡 Why it mattersThe deal is saved on this device only. To open it again later, tap Saved, then Load.
🍋 ExampleIt is like saving a game. You can turn it off, come back later, and pick up where you stopped. But the save is only on this one phone or computer.
✅ Lesson 1 done. You learned: How to type a deal. How to read the margin and the score. That the gold box tells you what to offer. That a lower land price can fix a deal.
Lesson 2 · Step 2 in the app · Hire a GC

How do I pay my builder?

A GC is a general contractor. That is the builder who runs the job. You will compare four ways to pay one.

1Go to step 2 and check the job

👉 Do thisScroll to the bottom of step 1. Tap Next: How do I pay my builder? →. Look at the block 🧱 The job.
👀 You should seeCost of the work: 300,000, with a small tag that says from Spec Build.
💡 Why it mattersYou did not type this. The app did the math from lesson 1: $150 × 2,000 sqft = $300,000. Your numbers carry over.
🍋 ExampleYou already told the app how big the house is and what each square foot costs. It is like a waiter who remembers your order. You do not have to say it again.

2Look at the builder’s terms

👉 Do thisLook at the block 🤝 The GC’s terms. Do not change anything yet.
👀 You should seeGC fee 15%. Markups of 20%. A GMP cap of 396,175.
💡 Why it mattersThese are sample numbers. On a real deal, you type what your builder quoted you.
🍋 ExampleA baker says, “I charge $10 on top of what the ingredients cost.” That $10 is the baker’s fee. A builder’s fee works the same way, as a percent.

3Compare the four cards

👉 Do thisLook at the four cards on the answer side. Find the big you pay number on each one.
👀 You should see 🧾 Cost-plus: $397,750 ⭐ costs you least
🧢 GMP: $408,963
🔒 Lump sum: $437,400
📐 Design-build: $442,200
💡 Why it mattersIf the job goes as planned, cost-plus is the cheapest. But look at its line Most you can be billed: No limit.
🍋 ExampleYou hire a baker to make a cake. Ingredients should cost $30.
Cost-plus: you pay for the ingredients, plus $10. Today that is $40.
GMP: the same, but never more than $45.
Lump sum: the cake is $46, no matter what.
Design-build: the baker also designs the cake. $48, no matter what.

4Test a surprise

👉 Do thisIn the box 🤔 What if the job really costs, tap +10%.
👀 You should seeThe job now costs $357,500. That is $32,500 over. The ⭐ jumps from cost-plus to GMP.
Way to payOn budgetCosts 10% more
Cost-plus$397,750 ⭐$435,125
GMP$408,963$420,175 ⭐
Lump sum$437,400$437,400
Design-build$442,200$442,200
💡 Why it mattersWith cost-plus, you paid for the surprise. With GMP, the builder paid the part above the cap. Lump sum and design-build did not move at all. Cheapest is not always safest. Now tap On budget.
🍋 ExampleEggs and butter go up. The ingredients now cost $40, not $30.
Cost-plus: you now pay $50. The surprise was yours.
GMP: you pay $45, the cap. The baker pays the other $5.
Lump sum: still $46. Design-build: still $48.
The cheapest way changed from cost-plus to GMP.

5Practice with a linked box

👉 Do thisIn Cost of the work, type 320000
👀 You should seeThe tag changes to your number. The cards change too.
👉 Now do thisErase everything in that box. Tap outside the box.
👀 You should seeThe box goes back to 300,000. The tag says from Spec Build again.
💡 Why it mattersYou can type over any linked box to use your own number. Erase it to go back.
🍋 ExampleA linked box is like a mirror. It shows what step 1 shows. When you type over it, you cover the mirror with your own picture. Erase it and the mirror is back.
Did you notice the pink “over” on every card? Each card has a line called Against your Spec Build budget. On Maple Street they all say over. Why? In lesson 1, your build cost did not have a builder’s fee in it. This step only compares. It does not change step 1. When you pick a real builder, go back to step 1 and type the real cost there.
✅ Lesson 2 done. You learned: Your build cost carries over. There are four ways to pay a builder. The cheapest way can change when the job costs more. How linked boxes work.
Lesson 3 · Step 3 in the app · Lender Terms

What will my loan cost?

A lender is a bank, or anyone who loans you money. You will see what the bank pays and what you pay.

1Go to step 3 and read your loan

👉 Do thisScroll to the bottom of step 2. Tap Next: What will my loan cost? →. Leave Build & sell loan picked. Look at Your loan on the answer side.
👀 You should seeYour loan: $414,752. The bank pays 73%. You pay 27%.
💡 Why it mattersThe bank does not pay for everything. The gold part of the bar is your own money.
🍋 ExampleYou want to build a $100 lemonade stand. The bank says, “We will lend you 80%.” The bank gives you $80. You bring the other $20.

2See why the loan stops there

👉 Do thisScroll to the block ✅ The bank’s two tests.
👀 You should seeLoan to cost: $414,752. Loan to ARV: $576,000. The smaller one is your limit: $414,752.
💡 Why it mattersThe bank runs two tests. One uses what the job costs. One uses what the finished house is worth (that is the ARV). The smaller answer wins.
🍋 ExampleThe bank has two rules. Rule 1: “We lend up to 80% of what it costs.” The stand costs $100, so that is $80. Rule 2: “We lend up to 72% of what it will be worth.” It will be worth $150, so that is $108. The bank uses the smaller number: $80.

3Read your cash

👉 Do thisLook at the four tiles. Then scroll to the last line, Total cash you need.
👀 You should see Cash at closing: $45,045
You pay during the build: $73,688
Interest: $36,622
Cost of the money: $49,417
Total cash you need: $155,355
💡 Why it mattersClosing is the day you sign. You need $45,045 that day. You need $155,355 in all. Make sure you have it before you make an offer.
🍋 ExampleBorrowing is like renting money. The rent is called interest. Borrow $100 at 10% for one year and the rent is $10. A “point” is a fee you pay on day one. Two points on $100 is $2.

4Flip one switch

👉 Do thisIn the block 🏦 What the bank charges, tap the switch Interest reserve to turn it on.
👀 You should seeTotal cash you need drops to $126,894. Your loan grows to $444,976. Interest goes up to $37,780.
💡 Why it mattersWith a reserve, the bank keeps the interest inside the loan and pays itself. You need less cash from your pocket. But the loan is bigger, so you pay more interest. Now tap the switch again to turn it off.
🍋 ExampleYou owe the bank $10 in rent on your loan. With a reserve, the bank says, “We will lend you $10 more, and use it to pay ourselves.” You keep your $10 today. But now you owe $90, not $80.
✅ Lesson 3 done. You learned: How much the bank lends, and why. How much cash you need on signing day and in all. That one switch can change your cash. On a real deal, type the numbers from your lender’s written quote.
Lesson 4 · Step 4 in the app · LOI & Due Diligence

How do I make my offer?

LOI means “letter of intent.” It is your offer letter. Due diligence means doing your homework on the land.

1Go to step 4 and look at the checklist

👉 Do thisScroll to the bottom of step 3. Tap Next: How do I make my offer? →. Find the box Letter checklist on the answer side.
👀 You should see5 of 9 must-haves. It lists what is missing. Also look at Offer price: it already says 150,000, from lesson 1.
💡 Why it mattersA letter needs 9 things before you send it. The app counts them for you.
🍋 ExampleYou want to buy a friend’s bike. You hand them a note: “I would like to buy your bike for $50, if it passes a test ride.” That note is like an LOI. It is not the real sale yet.

2Fill in who and what

👉 Do this In Buyer, type Maple Street Builders LLC
In Seller, type Pat Seller
In Property, type 123 Maple Street
In What you plan to build, type one single-family home
In Title / escrow company, type Sample Title Company
👀 You should seeThe checklist climbs to 9 of 9 must-haves. Confetti falls.
🍋 ExampleA note that says “I will buy it” is not enough. It needs to say who is buying, who is selling, and which bike. A letter for land needs the same things.

3Read your letter

👉 Do thisScroll to the block ✉️ Your letter. Read the first few lines. Then tap Copy letter.
👀 You should seeYour names, the property, and the price of $150,000 are already in the letter. A message says Letter copied.
💡 Why it mattersThe letter wrote itself from the boxes you filled in. You can paste it into an email or a document.
🍋 ExampleIt is like a fill-in-the-blank story. You gave the names and the numbers. The app put them in the right blanks.

4Read the four tiles

👉 Do thisLook at the four tiles above the letter.
👀 You should see Deposit: $2,500
Land vs. finished value: 18.8%
At risk before the deposit goes hard: $5,000
At risk after: $7,500
💡 Why it matters“At risk” is money you can lose. Checking the land costs about $5,000 here. After your deposit is locked in (“goes hard”), you can lose the deposit too.
🍋 ExampleYou give your friend $5 to hold the bike for you. That is like earnest money. You also pay a bike shop $10 to check the brakes. If you walk away during the test week, you get the $5 back. You do not get the $10 back.

5Give yourself more time

👉 Do thisLook at 📅 Key dates and remember the date next to Due diligence ends. Then, in the block 📅 The dates, change Due diligence period from 60 to 90
👀 You should seeDue diligence ends moves 30 days later. Closing moves 30 days later too.
💡 Why it mattersMore days means more time to check the land before your deposit is locked in. Your dates will not match the picture. They count from the day you do this.
🍋 ExampleYour friend gives you one week to test the bike. You ask for two weeks. Now you have more time to find a problem before your $5 is locked in.

6Start your homework list

👉 Do thisScroll down to 3 · Due diligence checklist. Tap the first line, Sold comps and days on market.
👀 You should seeA check mark on that line. The count says 1 of 17 done.
💡 Why it mattersThere are 17 things to check before your deposit is locked in. On a real deal, tap each one only when you have really done it.
🍋 ExampleBefore you buy the bike, you check the tires, the brakes and the chain. You tick each one off a list. The land checklist is the same idea, with 17 things to check.
This letter is for practice only Do not send it. On a real deal, have a real estate attorney read your letter first. The part where the seller stops taking other offers is a real promise once signed.
✅ Lesson 4 done. You learned: The price carries over. The letter needs 9 must-haves and writes itself. More days push your dates later. The checklist tracks your homework.
